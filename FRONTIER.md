## 1. The question, in plain language

Colour every point of a very high-dimensional space with one of r colours. Must some colour class contain a
congruent copy of a given finite shape? Shapes for which the answer is always yes (for every r, in high
enough dimension) are called **Ramsey**. Ramsey shapes must lie on a sphere, and for fifty years the
conjecture was that every shape on a sphere is Ramsey. On 20 September 2026 Pálvölgyi posted a seven-point
shape on a circle that is not (arXiv:2609.23327). On 23 September 2026 OpenAI released an exact algebraic
test (result 172, which cites Pálvölgyi's paper): a shape is Ramsey exactly when a certain system of linear
equations has a solution, with coefficients in F ⊗_ℚ F, where F is the field generated by the coordinates.

So the subject has changed character. It is no longer "is Graham right?" but "what does the algebraic test
say about natural families of shapes, and how is it decided in practice?" The answers so far mix
transcendence (whether a coordinate is algebraic), conformal geometry (Möbius maps preserve the answer), and
commutative algebra (what happens infinitesimally along the diagonal of F ⊗ F).

### What is now known (one paragraph each)

- **Möbius invariance** `[LEAN]`. Ramsey-ness of a spherical set is unchanged by Möbius maps of the sphere;
  on a circle it depends only on cross-ratios (Theorem C, mobius-ramsey).
- **Two groups suffice** (Theorem C2: `[CERT]` + glue for spheres up to S⁵, `[HAND]` in every dimension,
  AI-reviewed). The union of two Möbius images of algebraic sets is Ramsey.
- **Quadric independence** (`[LEAN]`, OpenAI's own Lean theorem `spherical_quadraticIndependent_ramsey_all`,
  Prop. 7.3 of result 172; prior work). If the points impose independent conditions on quadrics — no linear
  relation Σ λᵢ pᵢpᵢᵀ = 0 with pᵢ = (1, aᵢ) — the set is Ramsey. On a circle that means at most five points;
  on S^{d−1}, up to (d+1)(d+2)/2 − 1 points with algebraically independent coordinates (SR-C29: corollaries
  of OpenAI's theorem with a genericity step, reviewed once by an AI agent). So seven to nine generic points
  of S² are Ramsey and are not two-group.
- **First-order rigidity** (SR-C34, `[HAND]`: written proof in the note since v1.1.1, independently
  reviewed twice by non-author AI agents). If the dual space Δ(A) of the second-order ("2-jet") system vanishes, A is Ramsey. It contains quadric independence (no
  relations) and the note's rank criterion (circle, one parameter). Novel as far as found (sweep of
  2026-10-15); its algebraic-coordinate and small-point-count cases are prior (OpenAI Prop. 7.3; JD-Jones-ASES).
- **Obstructions** come from quadric relations combined with derivations of the reals (Pálvölgyi's
  Lemma A.1; the 2-jet test of the note, a necessary condition, is its matrix-weighted generalization).
  At quadric corank one the two coincide (SR-C31(a), `[HAND]`, reviewed twice, AI); at corank three and four on S²
  there are 2-jet failures with no scalar certificate (SR-C32, proved for four seeded families).
- **Thresholds.** On the circle: 7 points can fail (6 cannot); radian progressions {kθ} are Ramsey with 8
  points for every θ (`[HAND]`, AI-reviewed) and fail with 9 exactly when e^{iθ} is transcendental (Theorem D
  `[LEAN]`). For generic points of S^{d−1}, d ≥ 3, the threshold is (d+1)(d+2)/2: Ramsey below it by
  OpenAI's criterion, not Ramsey at it by Pálvölgyi's appendix Theorem A.12 (labelled unchecked by its author).

## 3. The ranked frontier

Ranking weighs interest against the sharpness of the first step. Difficulty: **A** attackable with the
release's tooling, **M** needs new mathematics, **E** engineering or review.

### F1. When does the 2-jet decide? (SR-C34, SR-C31) — A/M, interest high

**Plain.** Infinitesimally, a Ramsey certificate is built from a conformal vector field (an infinitesimal
Möbius motion) plus a quadric correction. Counting how many such pieces exist against how many equations the
points impose gives a prediction of which shapes are Ramsey, valid on every sphere.

**Precisely.** Let κ be the quadric corank of A ⊂ S^{d−1} (the dimension of the space of relations
Σ λᵢ pᵢpᵢᵀ = 0), k the Möbius-reduced transcendence degree of its coordinates, and G = d(d+1)/2 = dim so(1,d).
- *Rigidity* (SR-C34, `[HAND]`, written proof in the note since v1.1.1, reviewed twice, AI). If the 2-jet dual space Δ(A) is
  zero, A is Ramsey (Smith form over (F⊗F)_𝔪, the duality behind the 2-jet test, Nakayama). It is silent
  when κ ≥ 1 and some derivation is Möbius-null (for example transcendental rotations of rational sets).
- *Corank law* (SR-C31(b), consistent on sample). For configurations in general position within their
  family, the 2-jet test fails iff κ = 1 and k > G, or κ ≥ 2 and κ(k+1) > 2G. On the circle this is the
  ledger's expected-dimension law SR-C23; on S² it predicts the thresholds (κ, first failing k) = (1, 7),
  (2, 6), (3, 4), (4, 3).
- *One-parameter threshold* (consistent on sample). Together these suggest: one-parameter configurations
  of S^{d−1} in general position are Ramsey iff they have fewer than (d+1)² points — 9 on the circle
  (Theorem D), 16 on S².

**Known.** Exact checks on 38 seeded rational-family configurations on S¹, S², S³: every predicted threshold
is where the exact dual dimension changes, and an independent modulo-p primal check agrees on 8/8. For one
parameter on S², exact dual dimensions, the truncated π-adic test (order 12, modulo p) and the 2-jet test
agree with "Ramsey iff fewer than 16 points" on 10/10, and two 16-point configurations are certified
non-Ramsey by an exact 16 × 16 determinant `[CERT]`. Where Δ = 0 at a specialization of maximal quadric
rank, the Ramsey verdict is a theorem by SR-C34; on S² this gives Ramsey sets with one to four quadric
relations, which on the sample are not two-group.

**First step.** A lemma: for a rational family in general position, the map (C^ρ) ↦ Σ_ρ Π_{λ^ρ} C^ρ has the
expected rank and the pairing is generically nonzero; then the (d+1)² law becomes a theorem. (The
literature sweep for SR-C34 was done on 2026-10-15: novel as far as found.) **Tools:** pen and paper; sympy/python-flint. **Audience:**
commutative algebraist; Lean formalizer (it is pure algebra over OpenAI's `FieldCriterion`).

### F2. Second-order completeness (SR-C1) — M, interest high

**Plain.** Is the infinitesimal (second-order) test always decisive?

**Precisely.** A is Ramsey iff its 2-jet system is solvable. Necessity is `[HAND]` (AI-reviewed).
Reformulation (SR-C33(a)–(c), `[HAND]`, written proof in the note since v1.1.1, reviewed twice, AI): a set A
that affinely spans is Ramsey iff a vector b lies
in Φ(𝔪 O^M) over O = (F⊗F)_𝔪, so each A has *some* finite jet order that decides it (Krull intersection
theorem; not effective); SR-C1 claims order 3 always suffices. With SR-C34, the open cases are exactly
those where the 2-jet dual space is nonzero but every dual pairs to zero. In corank one and one parameter
the only open case is "a derivation satisfies Lemma A.1's linear conditions and its energy E vanishes".

**First step.** Construct such doubly degenerate corank-one families (for example 10 points on S² with one
parameter, chosen so that a Lemma A.1 derivation exists with E ≡ 0) and compute the π-adic orders of the
ideal of maximal minors and of the obstruction. **Tools:** exact algebra over ℚ(T)[U] localized at U = T (the
release's `exact_circle_cert.py` generalizes). **Audience:** algebraist, computation.

### F3. Non-Ramsey sets without a scalar certificate (SR-C32, SR-C35; answers SR-C11) — A, interest high

**Plain.** Every non-Ramsey spherical set known before v1.1.0 has a *scalar* certificate: one quadric
relation and one discontinuous derivation of the reals, whose "derivative energy" |∂x|² gives a colouring
(Pálvölgyi's Lemma A.1). We found sets that fail the second-order test but have no such scalar certificate.
A matrix-weighted version of the same colouring still defeats them.

**Precisely.** For 12 points on S² with stereographic coordinates rational-affine in 4 independent
parameters (quadric corank 3), and 13 points with 3 parameters (corank 4), four seeded families: the 2-jet
test fails and no relation λ and derivation satisfy Lemma A.1's linear conditions, even over ℂ (SR-C32,
proved for those families: `[CERT]` plus glue, reviewed by one non-author AI agent; non-Ramsey via the
2-jet necessity). These are the first non-Ramsey sets known to us with no scalar (single-relation)
derivation certificate. The two counts first separate on S² at (κ, k) = (3, 4); on the circle they never do.
A reviewer derived a matrix-weighted Lemma A.1 (SR-C35, `[SKETCH]`): a nonzero pairing on the 2-jet dual
gives an explicit colouring with M^N colours, which would make these sets non-Ramsey without the 2-jet
necessity. "Matrix weights are strictly stronger" is true for certificates, not (as far as is known) for
colourings.

**First step.** Review the matrix-weighted Lemma A.1 (SR-C35) and determine its colour counts. **Tools:** pen
and paper; EGMRSS Lemma 15. **Audience:** combinatorialist.

### F4. Measurable Graham (SR-C5, M1) and the trapezoid density question — M, interest highest, tractability low

**Plain.** Pálvölgyi's counterexample needs the axiom of choice: its colourings are wildly
non-measurable. Is every spherical set Ramsey for *measurable* colourings?

**Precisely.** SR-C5 (M1, M1w, M1cat). Measurable derivations are zero, so the known obstruction mechanism
dies for measurable data; o-minimal colourings are trivially fine (SR-C25), and so are colourings through
boundedly many polynomials (SR-C26). No Euclidean density theorem is known for any affinely dependent,
non-product spherical set.

**First step.** Literature and a test case: an exact density (or measurable) Euclidean Ramsey theorem for a
single isosceles trapezoid in ℝ^D. The finite-field analogue is known (Lyall–Magyar–Parshall,
arXiv:2301.11306); a Euclidean adaptation is the natural lever. **Tools:** harmonic analysis.
**Audience:** analyst, combinatorialist, set theorist (consistency of ZF + DC + "no discontinuous
homomorphisms" + ¬Graham).

### F5. Nine or more points on a circle (SR-C6, SR-C23 at k ≥ 2) — A (one parameter), M (general), interest medium-high

**Plain.** On a circle, seven and eight points can be Ramsey for "exotic" reasons (the note's NTG sets); at
nine points every exotic family seems to die.

**Precisely.** SR-C6: for n ≥ 9 cyclic points, positive-dimensional Ramsey families are two-group. With the
corank language: κ = n − 5 ≥ 4 forces the 2-jet to fail for every k ≥ 1 in general position. Known: exact
nonzero 9 × 9 determinants for 176 one-parameter non-two-group samples; rigid-group absorption (SR-C14).

**First step.** One parameter: prove "nine non-two-group cyclic points ⇒ the 9 × 9 evaluation determinant
over ℚ(T, U) is nonzero", i.e. nine points (sᵢ(T), sᵢ(U)) lie on a common (2,2)-curve only if they lie on
two Möbius graphs. **Tools:** algebraic geometry of (2,2)-curves on P¹ × P¹ (QRT-type correspondences).
**Audience:** algebraic geometer.

### F6. The phase diagram in every dimension (SR-C17, with SR-C29 and SR-C30) — E/A, interest medium

**Plain.** Take m rational points and j "free" points on a sphere. When is the union Ramsey?

**Precisely.** Conjecture SR-C17: non-Ramsey iff j ≥ 4 and m + j ≥ (d+1)(d+2)/2. Status now:
- the passing side is proved: j ≤ 3 by the two-group theorem, and m + j below the threshold as a corollary
  of OpenAI's Lean quadric-independence theorem (SR-C29); so are SR-C16(a) (seven generic points of S² are
  Ramsey), SR-C3 (generic thresholds, mostly prior work) and the refutation of SR-C7 (specialization
  closure) on S^{d−1} for every d ≥ 3;
- the all-free cell is Pálvölgyi's Theorem A.12 (appendix, labelled unchecked by its author);
- the mixed failing cells: SR-C30 (`[SKETCH]`, reviewed once by an AI agent: sound): embed the certified
  S² cell in a great 2-sphere and specialize. Exact per-instance certificates of the minimal cell for
  specific rational points, d = 3, …, 7. (Heuristic: four free points carry j(j − 3)/2 = 2 Möbius moduli,
  which is why the free-point threshold is 3 in every dimension.)

**First step.** A written proof of SR-C30's step-2 rank lemma (the stabilizer so(W^⊥) of the 2-sphere
through the four free points). **Tools:** exact
linear algebra. **Audience:** reviewer; computation.

### F7. Decidability (SR-C19) — A, interest medium

**Plain.** Given exact coordinates, can a computer decide Ramsey-ness?

**Precisely.** Over an explicitly presented finitely generated field, yes in principle: compute the syzygy
module of the evaluation map over ℚ[x, x′]/(I(x) + I(x′)) and test one linear condition over F (SR-C19;
SR-C33(a) gives the local form). The one-parameter circle case is implemented with 5/5 exact controls.
F1 suggests that on a dense open set the decision is a single linear-algebra computation (the 2-jet dual).

**First step.** Write the algorithm out with full proofs and implement general k in Singular or Macaulay2;
measure where the 2-jet already decides. **Audience:** computational algebra.

### F8. Formalization menu (Lean 4 on top of OpenAI's public proof) — E, interest medium

1. **Non-vacuity of Theorem D without Lindemann–Weierstrass.** Mathlib knows that Liouville numbers are
   transcendental. Taking tan(θ/2) = Σ 10^{−n!} gives an explicit non-Ramsey radian 9-gon in Lean today (the
   θ = 1 instance waits for Lindemann–Weierstrass in Mathlib). Small.
2. **Seven generic points of S² are Ramsey** (SR-C16, SR-C29): a corollary of OpenAI's
   `spherical_quadraticIndependent_ramsey_all` plus one exact rank-7 rational instance and a generic-rank
   lemma. Small; it would give a Lean-checked Ramsey set on S² that is not a union of two Möbius images of
   algebraic sets.
3. **The two-group theorem on the circle** (SR-C2, d = 2): the six-point certificate algebra already works
   over any commutative ring; estimated under 400 lines. Uniform-in-d over Mathlib's `CliffordAlgebra` is
   the larger version.
4. **The non-two-group certificates** (Theorem NTG): explicit (2,2)-forms over ℚ(T)[U]; mostly
   `linear_combination` on large polynomials.
5. **The 2-jet test** (Proposition J2) and first-order rigidity (SR-C34): about 60 lines of definitions,
   then algebra over OpenAI's `FieldCriterion`.

### F9. Locally semialgebraic colourings (SR-C28) — M, interest medium

**Plain.** The tame colourings that can still detect sphericity. **First step:** a SAT search over
ℤ^D-periodic grid colourings at small (D, cell size, r), constrained by exactly certified copies of the
radian 9-gon; any colouring surviving it must refine below scale D^{−1/2} (cube lemma). **Audience:**
computation; model theorist.

### F10. Colour numbers (SR-C21) and Ramsey dimension (SR-C20) — A (upper bounds), M, interest medium

How many colours does a non-Ramsey set need, and how large must the dimension be for a Ramsey one?
**First step:** the bookkeeping of colour counts in the Lemma A.1 route (EGMRSS Lemma 15) for the circle
heptagons, the radian 9-gon and the S² cells, and in the matrix-weighted route (SR-C35) for the sets of F3.

### F11. Three Möbius groups on the circle (SR-C15) — A, interest medium

Generic unions of three rigid four-point groups fail the 2-jet `[PROBE]`. **First step:** exact Fredholm
duals for (4,4,1) and (4,4,4) with the release's `dual_cert.py` machinery; the corank count of F1 predicts
which cells fail.

### F12. Transcendence-hard instances (SR-C9) — A (literature), interest medium

The nine points at 0, e, 2e, …, 8e radians are Ramsey iff e^{ie} is algebraic, which is open as far as we
know (Schanuel's conjecture would settle it). **First step:** a careful literature check of the status of
e^{ie}; then a list of "explicit configurations whose Ramsey-ness is equivalent to a named transcendence
problem". **Audience:** transcendence theorist.

### F13. Specialization on the circle, hyperbolic and pseudo-Euclidean analogues (SR-C7, SR-C18, SR-C8) — A/M

Successor to SR-C7: on the circle, is the Ramsey locus of an irreducible family of cyclic configurations
closed under specialization? (Off the circle it is not: SR-C7 is refuted for every d ≥ 3.) In hyperbolic space, are the Ramsey sets exactly the horospherical models of
Euclidean ones? **First steps:** degenerations of two-group families with the exact one-parameter
procedure; the horocyclic triple {0, 1, 2} as a test case for SR-C18.

## 4. Corrections and credit relative to v1.0.0

The full list is `CHANGELOG.md`. In short:
- SR-C16(a) is a corollary of OpenAI's quadric-independence theorem (Prop. 7.3 of result 172); the v1.0.0
  remark that a certificate would be "the first certified Ramsey set beyond the two-group mechanism" on a
  higher sphere was wrong and is withdrawn.

  the successor question of F13. SR-C3 is proved, mostly by prior work.

  dimension) are now cited.

  automatic and is re-scoped.
